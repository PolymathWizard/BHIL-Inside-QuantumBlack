# IP firewall

This study teaches the discipline of a communication system without
licensing anyone to impersonate its owner. The firewall has four walls,
and the build recipes repeat them because repetition is the point.

## Wall 1: structural extraction only

The library records structure, sequence, chart types, framework names as
competitive facts, and phrase patterns. It never reproduces paragraphs.
The quotation budget is one illustrative quotation of at most 25 words per
artifact, cited by URL. Visual examples in the companion field guide are
BHIL reconstructions or study excerpts used for analysis.

## Wall 2: no passing off

Nothing generated from these recipes may claim, imply, or simulate
McKinsey or QuantumBlack authorship, branding, or affiliation. Bylines on
generated work name the actual authors.

## Wall 3: no trademarked names

Named constructs observed in the corpus are recorded here as competitive
facts. Replication invents original names. The recipes ship with a
do-not-copy list: exact logos, trademarked framework names, exact page
layouts, exact trade dress.

## Wall 4: no trade-dress replication

Color behavior, typography register, and layout laws are recorded as
observations. Your artifacts use your identity. The test is simple: a
reader who knows both brands should recognize a shared level of craft and
zero shared costume.

## Why the firewall is also good design advice

Copying a costume caps you at imitation quality and creates legal risk.
Learning the underlying moves, answer-first headlines, one idea per
exhibit, visible methodology, lets you exceed the source on your own
terms. Use the method as a quality bar, not as a costume.
