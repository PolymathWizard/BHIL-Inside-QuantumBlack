# Density by artifact type

The visual rules stay stable, but the amount of evidence per page changes
by artifact class. Matching density to artifact type is itself a design
decision the corpus makes consistently.

## The three density classes

| Class | Typical visual load | Text and visual balance | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Short article | 1 to 3 exhibits | Text-led | One decision or one framework |
| Survey report | 8 to 15 exhibits | Balanced to visual-led | Findings, cohorts, comparisons |
| Trend compendium | Dozens of scorecards | Dense and modular | Repeated category profiles |

## The STENCIL benchmark

Survey reports place roughly one exhibit every one to two pages. Short
articles are more text-led. The trend compendium runs a fixed scorecard
per chapter so density stays high without ever feeling improvised. Tier:
CORROBORATED from page counts across the register.

## What density signals

Density is a promise about reading mode. A short article promises a
single sitting and one takeaway. A survey report promises skimmability by
exhibit. A compendium promises reference use, dip in anywhere, and every
chapter behaves the same. Mismatched density breaks the promise: a
20-exhibit article exhausts, a 2-exhibit survey report underdelivers.

## Applying the classes

Decide the artifact class before drafting, then hold the density budget.
When the draft wants more exhibits than the class allows, the material is
telling you it belongs in a different class, split the article or promote
it to a report rather than breaking the budget.
