# Credibility devices

Methodology is part of the visual system. Research credibility is visible
on the page, not hidden in an appendix.

## The four devices

1. **Method box.** Field dates, sample size, weighting, and definitions
   stated plainly, usually as a sidebar near the front or back.
2. **Source line.** Each exhibit names its dataset or evidence source.
3. **Qualification.** Self-reported results, forecast bands, and
   historical records carry visible limits where they appear.
4. **Named authorship.** Bylines and office locations attach
   accountability to the interpretation.

## The about-the-research standard

State the field period, respondent count, geographic coverage, weighting
approach, and the exact population behind each exhibit. When a chart
switches populations, say so on the chart, not three pages later.

## Why designers should own this

Credibility devices are layout elements. If the method box has no
designed home, it gets cut in production and the artifact ships as
assertion. Giving methodology a fixed, styled slot is the design decision
that makes the honesty durable.

## The BHIL extension

This repo goes one step further than the observed system: every claim
carries an evidence tier, firm self-claims are quarantined as STATED, and
no tool ever reports CLEAN because absence of anomaly is INCONCLUSIVE.
Those rules are BHIL discipline, not McKinsey practice, and they are the
reason this guide can cite itself. See
[evidence discipline](../method/evidence-discipline.md).
