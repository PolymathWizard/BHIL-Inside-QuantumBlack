# The case pattern

Case narratives simplify the argument into a before, intervention, and
result arc. The client is the hero; the firm is the guide.

## The five-beat arc

1. **Situation.** A named or anonymized client faces a clear operating
   problem.
2. **Complication.** The old process is slow, fragmented, or difficult to
   scale.
3. **Capability built.** A system, workflow, or model is tied directly to
   the problem.
4. **Measured result.** A before-and-after callout gives the reader a
   concrete outcome.
5. **Sustainable close.** The story ends with the capability that remains
   after the engagement.

## Why the arc works

The hero structure lets a reader project themselves into the client seat.
The capability close reframes the engagement from services rendered to
capacity transferred, which is the story every buyer wants to retell
internally.

## Evidence caution

The source register marks several QuantumBlack case cards as thin
marketing evidence, held at UNCORROBORATED, and the case-pattern finding
itself rests on fewer than three solid artifacts, which makes it
INFERENCE by rule. Treat the structure as observable, but keep outcome
claims at their stated evidence level. Any before-and-after number in a
case card is STATED until independently checked.

## Building your own case narratives

Use the five beats. Quantify the result only when you can source it, and
put the qualifier on the page. A modest verified number beats an
impressive unverifiable one in every audience that matters.
