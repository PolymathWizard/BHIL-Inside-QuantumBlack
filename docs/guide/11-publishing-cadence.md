# Publishing cadence

The franchise repeats, the vocabulary moves. Annual reports create a
stable container for a changing market question.

## The flagship series

The State of AI series publishes on an annual rhythm: an adoption-focused
edition, then a rewiring edition, then an agents edition, then a
return-on-investment edition. Each carries the same skeleton: answer-first
opening, exhibit sequence, cohort or framework reveal, methodology, named
authorship. The trend compendium runs its own mid-year rhythm with a
scored-chapter format now in its fifth edition.

## What stays fixed

Answer-first opening, exhibit sequence, cohort or framework reveal,
methodology sidebar, named authorship. A returning reader needs zero
onboarding.

## What moves

The headline vocabulary tracks the next executive question: adoption,
rewiring, agents, then return on investment. Even the recurring anchor
statistic is a cadence device: repeating a stable impact figure across
editions turns it into a series-long tension the next edition promises to
address.

## Why cadence is a design asset

A predictable franchise trains the market to expect the artifact, lets
the design system compound instead of resetting, and makes year-over-year
deltas a native chart type. The container does marketing work the content
never has to.

## Adopting cadence honestly

Commit to a named, dated series only if you can re-field it. A franchise
that skips a year spends its accumulated trust. Fix the methodology
sidebar in edition one so later editions can say "up from last year" with
a straight face. Tier: VERIFIED for the cadence itself, dated editions
sit in the register.
