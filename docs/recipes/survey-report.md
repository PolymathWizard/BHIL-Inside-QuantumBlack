# Recipe: survey report

Quantify the field, show what matters, and prove it. This is the seven-move
skeleton extracted from the flagship survey franchise, rebuilt as a recipe
for your own benchmark series.

## The skeleton

1. **Answer-first title.** Lead with the conclusion, not the process.
2. **Key takeaways.** Three to five bullets, insight-led, not procedural.
3. **Quantified lede.** Open with a data point that sets the stakes,
   ideally a tension between two numbers.
4. **Exhibit sequence.** Visuals that advance the argument, not decorate
   it. Numbered, one idea each, conclusion titles throughout.
5. **Cohort reveal.** Segment the data to make the story visible, the
   small group doing it differently is the narrative engine.
6. **Methodology note.** Source, sample, and approach, clearly stated:
   field dates, n, coverage, weighting.
7. **Byline.** Named authors with accountability, not a house brand.

## Density budget

Eight to fifteen exhibits, balanced to visual-led, roughly one exhibit
every one to two pages. If the draft wants more, split the report.

## Franchise rules

Name the series, date the editions, fix the methodology in edition one,
and re-field annually so edition two can quantify change. Do not brand a
one-off as a franchise.

## Guardrail

Original series name, original construct names, your visual identity.
Do not copy exact logos, trademarked framework names, exact page layouts,
or exact trade dress. Nothing in the output claims or implies McKinsey or
QuantumBlack authorship.

## Acceptance test

Read only the exhibit titles. If they tell the whole argument in order,
ship. If any title names a chart instead of stating a finding, fix it
before layout.
