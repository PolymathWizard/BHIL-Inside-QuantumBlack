# LinkedIn launch posts

Three variants per BHIL convention: technical authority, inquiry-driving,
and peer builder ship post. Story over statistics; each closes with the
tagline.

## Variant 1: technical authority

I just published an open repository that deconstructs how McKinsey
QuantumBlack communicates on the page.

Not the advice. The architecture. The way a headline carries the answer,
the way every exhibit makes exactly one point, the way methodology sits
on the page instead of in an appendix, the way named frameworks arrive
only after the evidence has made them feel inevitable.

The repo turns those observations into build recipes a design team can
run: a survey report skeleton, a paradox essay skeleton, a trend
compendium skeleton, and a production checklist. Every claim carries an
evidence tier, every derived file sits behind a drift gate, and every
recipe carries a hard rule: original names, original identity, no trade
dress. Study the craft, not the costume.

Human-Directed. AI-Enabled. Commercially Tested.

## Variant 2: inquiry-driving

Why can you read a QuantumBlack report in ninety seconds and still walk
away with the argument?

I spent a research cycle finding out, and the answer is smaller than you
would expect. A handful of rules, applied without exception. The headline
is the answer. The exhibit is the atomic unit. The framework waits for
the evidence. The method stays visible.

I have opened the whole study as a repository for designers: the field
guide, the build recipes, the evidence register behind every claim. If
your team produces research artifacts and you want them to read faster
without shouting, the repo is linked in the comments.

What is the one rule your publishing system applies without exception?

Human-Directed. AI-Enabled. Commercially Tested.

## Variant 3: peer builder ship post

Shipped: BHIL-Inside-QuantumBlack.

A git repo for designers who want to understand how a top AI consulting
practice communicates strategically, built from a 32-artifact STENCIL
deconstruction.

Inside: a twelve-chapter field guide, three build recipes plus a
checklist, a drift-gated source register, stdlib-only Python gates, CI
workflows, and ADRs with their open questions left open on purpose.
Regression tests name the bugs they prevent. No tool in the repo is
allowed to say CLEAN, because absence of anomaly is inconclusive.

The IP firewall is part of the design: structure is learnable, trade
dress is not, and the recipes enforce the difference.

Human-Directed. AI-Enabled. Commercially Tested.
